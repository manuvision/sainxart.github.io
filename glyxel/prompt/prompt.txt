# Updated Consistent Prompt — 3-Color Sketchy Pixel Creature Sheet

Create a **single clean creature concept sheet** based on the attached **8×8 pixel image**.

The result must be **highly consistent in structure every time**. Do **not** improvise the page layout. Follow the required layout and content below exactly.

## Main goal

Interpret the attached pixel image as the seed for a **monster / creature / character**.

The pixel image should guide the creature’s main design logic — overall silhouette, head shape, horn or antenna placement, body mass, limb rhythm, appendages, facial structure, armor-like areas, markings, or structural balance — but it should **not** be copied too literally.

The final creature should feel like a **designed being**, not an abstract symbol, not a voxel figure, and not a rigid blocky pixel creature.

## Important interpretation rule for the pixel seed

Treat the **colored pixels** as the main positive mass of the creature.

Treat the **black pixels / black background** as **transparency, empty space, or void**. These negative spaces should help define the overall silhouette and structure of the creature.

Use the pixel design as an **approximately 80% guide**:

* keep the spirit and major proportions of the pixel seed
* preserve the overall visual logic
* let the negative space help define the shape
* but do **not** follow it so literally that the creature ends up with awkward square limbs, rigid block growths, or overly geometric anatomy

The final design should feel **interpreted, intentional, and readable**, not mechanically traced.

## Overall creature direction

The creature should feel:

* compact and stylized
* proportionally **chibi-like** in the sense of **large head / compact body / readable silhouette**
* **not necessarily cute**
* sometimes cute, sometimes eerie, sometimes noble, sometimes villainous, depending on what is suggested by the pixel seed
* simple enough for a beginner to redraw with markers
* visually interesting, but not overdesigned

“Chibi” here refers mainly to the **proportions**, not to a mandatory cute personality.

## Design interpretation rule

Do **not** assume the creature must be organic or biomimetic every time.

Instead, let the **shape itself** suggest the direction. Depending on the pixel seed, the creature may lean toward:

* organic
* biomimetic
* plant-like
* animal-like
* nature-spirit-like
* techno-organic
* robotic
* cybernetic
* relic-based
* artifact-inspired
* solarpunk
* ancestral-future
* or a hybrid of several of these

The pixel seed should determine which of these influences feels most natural.

## Visual style

The whole page should look like a **minimalist hand-drawn creature design sheet** with a **sketchy doodle style**.

The final aesthetic should be closer to a **loose marker doodle / rough concept sketch** than to polished fantasy illustration.

Use:

* loose hand-drawn linework
* sketchy outlines
* visible doodle energy
* simple marker fills
* warm off-white / beige sketchbook paper
* a raw, spontaneous artist-sketch feel
* hand-drawable, approachable design
* subtle Copic-marker spirit

Avoid:

* over-rendering
* realism
* highly polished concept-art finish
* dense texture
* heavy lighting
* complex shading
* detailed blending
* glossy fantasy rendering

The result should feel like something a beginner could actually **copy by hand with a few markers**.

## Required fixed layout

The sheet must contain **exactly these three visual stages**, arranged **from left to right**:

### 1) Left: Barebones silhouette / shape sketch

A **single very simple rough monochrome sketch** based directly on the pixel image.

Requirements:

* black / gray pencil or ink only
* no color
* minimal detail
* primitive shape exploration only
* clearly informed by the pixel arrangement
* should emphasize the rough shape logic and void / negative-space structure
* this is the rawest version on the page

### 2) Middle: Advanced completed sketch

A **single more developed monochrome sketch** of the same creature.

Requirements:

* still uncolored
* cleaner and more readable than the first sketch
* standard neutral mostly symmetrical pose
* front-facing or near-front-facing
* clearly shows the resolved final creature design
* should still feel sketchy and hand-drawn
* should clearly be the same creature as the final illustration
* keep it simple, stylized, and beginner-friendly

### 3) Right: Final colored illustration

A **single final colored illustration** of the same creature.

Requirements:

* dynamic pose
* same creature as the left and middle drawings
* compact stylized proportions
* sketchy doodle-style finish
* expressive and visually appealing
* simple enough for a beginner to redraw
* can be cute, neutral, eerie, or villainous depending on the creature
* use only the palette colors from the source pixel image
* flat marker-style fills
* no blending required
* no extra colors
* no soft gradients
* minimal shading only if it can be done with simple flat color blocking

The final drawing should feel like a **lively doodled marker creature**, not a polished trading-card illustration.

## Color rules

These are very important:

* The current pixel format uses **exactly 3 colors**
* Use **exactly those 3 colors from the source pixel image**
* Do **not** introduce extra accent colors
* Do **not** rely on blending
* Do **not** use gradients
* Keep the final creature readable with **flat areas of color**
* Any small shading or emphasis must still be possible using only those 3 colors
* The creature should feel designed around that strict palette

## Top text

At the **top of the page**, include:

### Creature name

Invent a **random original creature name**.

The name should feel short, memorable, and suitable for a species, spirit, relic-being, guardian, villain, or techno-organic creature.

### Tagline

Directly under or beside the name, include **one short sentence** that hints at the creature’s defining power, role, behavior, or essence.

Examples:

* “Stores stormlight in its hollow crest.”
* “A root-fed hunter that slips through sacred marshes.”
* “Uses relic charge to bend living armor.”
* “Builds nests from warm metal and fallen bark.”

Keep it to **one short sentence only**.

## Bottom palette

At the **bottom of the page**, include a neat palette strip showing the **3 colors from the original pixel image**.

Requirements:

* exactly **3 swatches**
* each swatch labeled with a color name
* use simple, readable names based on the actual colors
* the colors must match the source pixel image
* keep the palette presentation clean and consistent

## Consistency rules

These are mandatory:

* Include **exactly 3 creature drawings total**
* one rough sketch on the left
* one refined monochrome sketch in the middle
* one final colored illustration on the right
* include creature name and tagline at the top
* include exactly 3 labeled palette swatches at the bottom
* do **not** add extra poses
* do **not** add back views
* do **not** add extra variations
* do **not** add bullet-point notes
* do **not** add lore paragraphs
* keep the page clean, simple, and repeatable every time

## Strong negative constraints

Avoid:

* voxel look
* blocky geometry
* overly literal tracing of the pixel silhouette
* awkward square limbs
* stiff geometric body shapes
* over-detailed fantasy rendering
* complicated texture
* official Pokémon-style polish
* polished collectible-card look
* hard-surface mecha detail unless the seed strongly suggests it
* scenic background illustration
* cute-only interpretation
* assuming every creature must be organic by default

## Final composition summary

The final page should always contain, in this exact order:

1. **Top:** creature name + one short tagline
2. **Left:** one rough monochrome silhouette / shape sketch
3. **Middle:** one refined monochrome completed sketch in a neutral symmetrical pose
4. **Right:** one final colored dynamic sketchy-doodle illustration of the same creature
5. **Bottom:** exactly 3 labeled color swatches from the original pixel palette

## Final instruction

Maintain this layout and structure **consistently across all generations**. The creature may change according to the pixel seed, but the **format must remain fixed**.

Most importantly, the creature should be:

* guided by the pixel seed, but not trapped by it
* informed by both colored pixels and black negative space
* about **80% faithful** to the original pixel logic
* compact in proportion
* sometimes cute, sometimes eerie, sometimes villainous
* sometimes organic, sometimes robotic, sometimes hybrid, depending on the shape
* sketchy and doodle-like
* easy for a beginner to redraw with **only 3 flat marker colors and no blending**
