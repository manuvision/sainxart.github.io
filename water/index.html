No global live meter separates AI’s water use from other computing. These counters turn published research into a continuous, clock-driven model.

01 / What we count

Operational water consumption from AI workloads worldwide: water consumed by data-center cooling and by the electricity they use. Manufacturing is excluded. Consumption refers to water removed from the immediate water environment, often through evaporation. It is different from withdrawal, which includes water subsequently returned.

02 / The annual anchor

Alex de Vries-Gao’s study in Patterns, published online December 17, 2025, estimates 312.5–764.6 billion liters for 2025. Our central scenario is the arithmetic midpoint, 538.55 billion liters. These are scenarios within a published estimate range, not confidence bounds or live observations.

The carbon and water footprints of data centers and what this could mean for artificial intelligence ↗

From 2025 onward, we hold the chosen annual total constant. This is a reference-rate scenario, not a claim that water use stopped growing. Water intensity, power sources, and cooling technology can change independently of AI demand.

Today = annual liters × elapsed seconds today ÷ seconds in the UTC year

Every counter recalculates from the device’s UTC clock. “Today” resets at 00:00 UTC worldwide. Leap years use their actual duration. There is no per-request telemetry and no assumption that all facilities run at an identical measured rate.

03 / Why history begins in 2012

There is no established single date when the first AI data center was invented. AI used shared computing infrastructure long before dedicated facilities. We choose January 1, 2012 as a calendar baseline for the modern deep-learning era associated with AlexNet. It is a display convention, not a facility’s opening date.

Krizhevsky, Sutskever & Hinton · ImageNet classification, 2012 ↗

For 2012–2024, we extrapolate backward from the chosen 2025 anchor using an assumed 50% annual growth rate. This is an illustrative assumption we selected, not a measured historical series or a growth rate established by the paper.

Annual liters(y) = 2025 anchor ÷ 1.5(2025 − y)
Cumulative = completed years + elapsed fraction of this year

History is especially uncertain: using 25%, 50%, or 100% historical growth produces about 2.57, 1.61, or 1.08 trillion liters through the end of 2025 at the central anchor. The lower/upper buttons vary the annual anchor only; they do not capture all historical uncertainty.

04 / Reading the vessel

The sphere’s filled volume represents the fraction of the current UTC day elapsed, against that day’s modeled total. Its fill resets at midnight. Ripples, reflections, and floating particles are illustrative. Pause motion freezes the artwork while the numerical estimates continue. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.

05 / Further context

Li et al. distinguish a projected 2027 AI water withdrawal of 4.2–6.6 billion cubic meters from consumption of 0.38–0.60 billion cubic meters. We do not substitute the larger withdrawal figure for consumption.

Making AI Less “Thirsty” · version 5, March 26, 2025 ↗

The IEA’s April 2026 outlook projects roughly a tripling of AI-focused data-center electricity use from 2025 to 2030. Electricity growth alone does not establish equivalent water growth, so we do not automatically apply it to this counter.
